 Section heading: Logical Operators.
 Text content: It is very important 
	 that users understand the logical operators AND , OR , 
	 and NOT . When a query is executed, the criteria entered for 
	 different fields (such as ToolSet and Tran Status ) 
	 takes an AND relationship, while the different criteria entered 
	 for a specific field takes an OR relationship. For example, 
	 if the user enters FX and Cash in the ToolSet field, and New and Validated in the Tran Status fields, then any transactions in the database that have a toolset 
	 value of FX OR Cash , AND a Tran Status of New OR Validated will be retrieved. To reiterate, · the 
	 different rows on the query screen are ” AND ed” together · the 
	 comma-separated values are “ OR ed” together To perform more 
	 complex queries, users may use additional logical operators such as · less 
	 than (<) · greater 
	 than (>) · not 
	 equal to (!) · “and” 
	 (+) Note: The 
	 less than and greater than operators only apply to fields involving 
	 numbers such as the deal tracking number or dates. The following are 
	 examples of using the logical operators: The first inclination 
	 of many users is to enter “> 1May015” and “< 1Jun15” , but this 
	 would not work because the two criteria would be OR ed 
	 together, making the combined criteria “greater than 1May15” OR “less 
	 than 1Jun15”, which would include every date possible! The “and” operator 
	 changes the default behavior of the “OR” relationship that exists..
