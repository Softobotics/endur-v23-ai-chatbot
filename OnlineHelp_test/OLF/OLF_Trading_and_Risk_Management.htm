TRADING AND RISK MANAGEMENT SYSTEM: terminology and concepts

Overview

This document answers the question, "What is a Trading and Risk Management system?"

Terminology

The following table contains a series of terms and definitions for trading and risk management, as they apply to the Open Link System.

Term

Definition

Trading

Deal Capture and Operational Processing.

Deal Capture

This term indicates the ability to model deal types and support deal entry.  

Model Deal Types

The system must record all attributes of a deal, such as the price, quantity, counterparty, start and end dates, and many more fields related to each trade.

Deal Entry

Deals can be entered manually (by traders) or automatically (via trade import processes).

Operational Processing

Operational processing starts once deals are captured.  The system sends a confirmation to the counterparty that describes all of the deal details.  The counterparty reviews the confirmation and acknowledges the details of the trade.  Since trades have future payments tied to future events, the system will record the future event, compute the payment, generate an invoice, and satisfy any accounting requirements. 

Risk Management

Risk Management means understanding your market risk, your exposure to price movements, from position management to the more advanced concept of Value-at-Risk (VaR). 

Position Management

The system produces a consolidated position report for all the deals in the system to give a measure of your market risk. 

Value-at-Risk

Value-at-Risk is a measure of your market risk condensed into one easy to understand number.  Value-at-Risk answers the question, "under the worst case scenario, what is the highest dollar amount that can be lost?”

Example Trade

Deal Capture and Operational Processing

On March 9th two parties, Party A and Party B, agree to a trade.  They decide to base a payment, to be made on December 15th, based on the price of Crude Oil on November 30.  They used the following rule to compute the payment:  For every dollar the price of crude is above $30 a barrel Party A gives Party B $10.  For every dollar the price of crude is below $30 Party B gives Party A $10.

Risk Management: Position Management and Value-at-Risk Actions

Date

Action
